# The brief

The dive, made again from nothing but code. This is the brief the build is
held to. Its shape is borrowed from the prompt of
[Noniv/abyssal-garden](https://github.com/Noniv/abyssal-garden) (a
real-time underwater scene in one HTML file): priorities in order, hard
constraints, a target, a scene list, controls, and two gates that decide
when it is done. What changed for this project is said where it changes.

---

Build "All the Way Down" again as a real-time scene that runs in the
browser: the visitor scrolls, and dives with Snap from the pier to the
vents and back, building a crabcrop URL on the way. It has to look
beautiful, read as a website, and run on a phone.

## Priorities (in this order when they conflict)

1. Visual quality
2. Stable frame pacing at the target
3. Small bundle size

## Constraints

- **Everything is made in code.** No texture, model, video, sound or font
  file, and nothing generated by another tool. That includes Snap: he is
  built from shapes and a crochet shader, to his character bible
  (`design/snap-world/snap-character-bible.md`). A picture the page needs
  is drawn by a shader or on a canvas at run time.
- **three.js is allowed** (the reference allows no engine). It is the only
  dependency, and it is bundled into the page. Nothing is loaded from
  another host.
- **WebGL 2**, not WebGPU as the reference asks. This is a website: it has
  to open on the phones and browsers its visitors have today.
- **The deliverable is one self-contained `index.html`.** Report its
  minified and gzipped size.
- **No per-frame allocation in the dive's own code**: no garbage that makes
  the frame stutter. Buffers, vectors and matrices are made once. Motion
  lives in shaders wherever it can.
- **It is still the website.** The words are real DOM, readable with the
  scene switched off: with reduced motion, without WebGL, or by a screen
  reader. Every claim on it comes from the live pages.

## Target

- A laptop with integrated graphics at 1080p, and a recent phone: 60 frames
  a second, reached by lowering first the resolution, then how much is
  drawn, in small steps.
- A desktop with a dedicated card: the full scene at its native resolution.
- Three detail levels (low, medium, high), picked on a first visit from
  what the device says about itself, and adjusted while it runs.

## Scene

One continuous world, laid out around the path the camera takes down:

- **The pier, from below**: pilings, the hull of the boat, the anchor chain
  going down, the surface seen from underneath with the sun in it
- **The shallows**: sunlit sand, caustics, sea grass, schools of fish
- **The kelp forest**: tall, dense, swaying kelp with gold and green
  specks, and shafts of light between the stalks
- **The reef**: corals, sponges, fans, ground cover in pink and purple,
  a manta passing over, a giant clam
- **The shipwreck**: a wooden hull on a ledge, encrusted, a light inside
- **The twilight zone**: almost no sun; jellyfish and plankton that make
  their own light; the visitor's lamp
- **The vents**: black chimneys, glowing cracks, smoke, tube worms, and the
  geode where the print lies
- **The way back up**, and the surface again
- Throughout: absorption that turns cyan to deep blue to black with depth
  and distance, rising bubbles, drifting particles, no visible edge of the
  world
- **Snap**, in every scene: he leads the way down, and he is who the story
  is about

## Controls

- Scroll (wheel, touch, keyboard) to dive: the page is as tall as the dive
  is deep
- Move the pointer, or drag on a touchscreen, to look around a little
- Five choices on the way down, each a part of the URL
- Sound off by default, one button to turn it on; everything it plays is
  synthesized
- Keys: `M` sound, `H` frame statistics

## Verification

- **Visual critic.** A separate agent that sees only the renders, never the
  code or the reasoning behind it. It is given fixed camera poses, one or
  more per zone, and frames of the scripted descent, to catch shimmer,
  aliasing, popping and banding. It returns concrete defects in order of
  importance. Iterate until it reports nothing above "minor".
- **Performance.** A scripted descent with a frame-time log: average, 99th
  percentile and worst frame, draw calls and triangles per zone, and the
  growth of the JavaScript heap over the run (it should not grow).

  The machine this is built on has no graphics card: it draws in software.
  So the look can be judged here, and the heap, the draw calls and the
  triangle counts can be measured here, but GPU frame times cannot. The
  page carries the benchmark (`dev.html`, key `B`) so they can be measured
  on real hardware, and the report says plainly which numbers came from
  where.

## Done when

Both gates pass. Deliver `index.html`, its size, the performance report
and the critic's last review.
